She was only 13 when she helped her father calculate how many guns the new generation of Spitfires and Hurricanes would need to bring down enemy aircraft. The pair used calculating machines, which were rudimentary computers, at the kitchen table of their London home. The new plane was to have four guns that fired 1,200 bullets a minute and many thought that this would be more than enough. Her father was not so sure. In June and July 1934 they worked at night to create the evidence that her father's boss needed to underwrite a large change in the design. They realised that 256 bullets would be required in two seconds to bring down an enemy bomber at the increased speeds of the new aircraft, and for this eight guns were required.
